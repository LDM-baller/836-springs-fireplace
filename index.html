The neighbours at 830

The Pollock-Krasner House and Study Center sits immediately up the road at 830 Springs Fireplace Road. Lee Krasner and Jackson Pollock bought the 1879 fisherman's house there in November 1945, with a $2,000 down payment lent by Peggy Guggenheim against future work. Pollock painted in an upstairs bedroom until 1946, when he had the barn moved to open up the view and began using it as a studio — the drip paintings, Autumn Rhythm, Convergence and Blue Poles among them, were made on its floor. He worked there until his death in 1956; Krasner used the same barn until hers in 1984. The property became a National Historic Landmark in 1994 and has been open as a museum since 1988.

Accabonac Harbor

The tidal harbor the property looks toward. Its salt marsh is among the least altered on eastern Long Island, which is why so much of the shoreline around it is under permanent protection rather than under houses.

Merrill Lake Sanctuary

Twenty-nine acres of sunlit salt marsh and oak hummock on the harbor, with the entrance on Springs Fireplace Road — one of the first preserves the Nature Conservancy's Long Island chapter ever took on. Osprey nest there; red fox, piping plover and least tern use it; sea lavender and marsh elder grow in it. A 1.3-mile trail runs out and back, dawn to dusk.

836 was Pollock and Krasner land

Confirmed by the owners from the deed chain: this property was part of the Pollock and Krasner holding, and Lee Krasner's signature appears on a deed in the title history. The barn that now contains the kitchen stands from that period. It is a different building from the studio barn at 830 — that one is the museum — but it is of the same place and the same years.
